# ClientForge — Offer Sheet (single source of truth)

**This is the only place the offer and its price are defined.** The cold call
script, the cold email, the DM, the walk-in pitch, the dashboard's quote
dropdown, and the public site all read from this file. When the offer changes,
it changes **here first**, and everything in §7 gets updated in the same sitting.

Why one file: before this existed there were three different prices live at the
same time — the site said one thing, `agency_offers.py` said another, and the
cold-call playbook said a third. A prospect who hears one number on the phone
and finds a different one on the site does not ask which is right. They stop
answering.

*Owner-edited. Last updated: 2026-09-08. Decided by the operator on 2026-09-08.*

---

## 0. The offer in one sentence

> **The website is free. The care plan is $129/mo, six-month minimum, cancel
> after. They own the code and the domain from day one.**

That's it. There is no build fee, no deposit, no setup charge, no tiers to walk
someone through on a call.

---

## 1. Why free

The build is **customer-acquisition cost, not revenue.** We have zero clients.
The single most expensive thing we own right now is the reason a stranger says
no, and price is that reason. Removing it entirely removes the objection
entirely — there is nothing left to negotiate and nothing to "think about."

What we're actually buying with a free build:

- A live reference in the niche we're working (Philly childcare first).
- A real testimonial to replace proof we don't have.
- A relationship that expands into automations, ads, and apps later — the rungs
  in §4, which are where the actual money is.
- A recurring $129 that starts the month the site goes live and doesn't stop.

**This is a deliberate land-grab price, not a valuation of the work.** §6 says
when to raise it.

---

## 2. What $129/mo includes — and what it does not

Written this precisely on purpose. An uncapped "we'll handle anything" retainer
at $129 loses money on the first client who asks for a lot, and then we resent
them.

### Included

| | |
|---|---|
| **Hosting & SSL** | Live, secure, monitored for uptime |
| **Edits** | **Up to 2 content edit requests per month**, 48-hour turnaround |
| **Form watching** | We monitor that inquiries actually deliver — the form silently breaking is the #1 way a small-business site fails |
| **Speed-to-lead** | Instant auto-reply to every new inquiry |
| **Missed-call text-back** | Missed call → automatic text within seconds |
| **Monthly report** | One page: inquiries in, where they came from |
| **Cancel** | Anytime after month 6 |

### Not included — these are quotes, not care-plan work

- A new page, a redesign, or a new section (that's a build)
- Ad spend or ad management (§4, rung 3)
- Anything needing a new integration or tool
- Edits 3+ in a month — either quote them or, if it's becoming the norm, that
  client should be on a higher plan. Don't absorb it silently.

**The build itself is free but not infinite:** one site, up to 5 pages, their
content. A full custom app or e-commerce build is §4 rung 4, scoped separately.

---

## 3. Terms

- **Build:** $0. Delivered in 7–14 days from receiving their content.
- **Monthly:** $129, first charge on the day the site goes live — not at signing.
  They never pay for something they can't see yet.
- **Minimum:** 6 months. Say it plainly on the call; don't bury it.
- **Ownership:** code, domain, and assets are theirs from day one. No hostage.
- **Cancellation:** anytime after month 6, they keep the site. We hand over
  hosting or park it wherever they want.

**Why 6 months and not month-to-month:** the free build only makes sense if it
amortizes. $129 × 6 = $774 — that's the build being paid for over half a year.
Month-to-month means someone takes a free site and leaves in week three.

**Why the first charge is at launch, not at signing:** it removes the last
hesitation ("what if you never finish it"), and it puts the pressure on us to
ship, which is where pressure belongs.

## 4. The expansion ladder — where the money actually is

$129 does not build a business. It builds a **roster**. Every rung below is sold
to an existing, happy client — never on a cold call.

| Rung | What | Price | Sold when |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | Website — free | $0 | The door. Always. |
| **2** | **Care plan** | **$129/mo** | Signed at the same time as the build |
| **3** | Automations — CRM pipeline, follow-up sequences, review requests, booking reminders, AI receptionist | $250–$1,100 one-time, may bump the monthly | After the site is live and they trust us |
| **4** | Ads management (Eco) | $250 / $450 / $750 per month, **client pays spend directly** | Only for businesses already spending. Never cold. |
| **5** | Apps / custom builds | Scoped per project | When an existing client asks. Never pitched. |

**Rule that does not bend: never discount the monthly to save a deal.** The build
is already free — there is nothing left to give. A client who has to be argued
into $129 churns at month three and costs more than they paid.

## 5. How to say it

### On the call (the shape, not a script to read)

> "The site itself I'm doing free — I'd rather earn the relationship than the
> deposit. What I charge for is keeping it working: a hundred twenty-nine a
> month covers hosting, any edits you want, and I watch that the form is
> actually delivering leads to you. Six months, then cancel whenever. And the
> site is yours either way — code, domain, all of it."

### The value anchor — use theirs, not ours

Never defend $129 on cost. Anchor it against **one customer of theirs.**

| Niche | One customer is worth | $129/mo reads as |
|---|---|---|
| **Daycare / childcare** | ~$10–13k/yr per enrolled child | One extra child pays for ~8 years |
| Roofing | $8–15k per job | One job pays for ~7 years |
| HVAC | $6–12k per install | One install pays for ~5 years |
| Med spa | $2–4k/yr per client | One client pays for ~2 years |
| Home services | $500–2k per job | One job pays for several months |

> "One extra kid enrolled is what, ten, twelve grand a year to you? This is
> fifteen hundred."

### Banned on the phone and in every email

`unlock` · `supercharge` · `game-changer` · `seamless` · `revolutionary` · `10x`
· `the power of` · `leverage` (as a verb) · `solutions`

Full list: `marketing/context/voice_guide.md`. Voice is the same everywhere:
direct, specific, honest builder. Real numbers or nothing.

## 6. When to raise the price

$129 is a starting-from-zero price. It is not permanent. **Raise it — for new
clients only, never retroactively — when any of these is true:**

- **5 paying clients.** The objection-killing job of a free build is done.
- **A real testimonial is on the site.** Proof replaces price as the thing that
  closes.
- **Support time exceeds ~2 hrs/month/client.** The plan is underpriced for what
  it actually costs to deliver.
- **A prospect says yes without hesitating, twice in a row.** Too cheap.

**The honest ceiling of this price:** $129 × 6 = $774 per client over the minimum
term. Ten clients is $1,290/mo. This price gets a roster and proof, not a living.
The living comes from rung 3 and 4 (§4) and from raising the plan once the proof
exists. Know that going in so month four isn't a surprise.

**Existing clients keep their price.** A client who took a chance on us before we
had proof is not the one who pays for our growth.

tiers ($300 / $700 / $1,400). The call offers the build free. That is not a
contradiction to fix — it is what makes the free build land as a real concession
instead of a discount bin. **But the monthly must match everywhere.** The site's
care-plan range must contain $129, or the follow-up email kills the deal.

*Site care plan currently reads "$75–$200/mo" — $129 sits inside it. In sync.*
