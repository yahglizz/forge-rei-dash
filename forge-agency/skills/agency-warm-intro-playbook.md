# ClientForge — Warm Intro Playbook (daycare network)

**The first channel, ahead of cold calling.** You are a licensed Philadelphia
childcare director. Every other director in this city is a peer, not a prospect,
and a peer conversation converts several times better than a cold dial — from a
warmer start, with no gatekeeper, and with proof they can drive to and look at.

Offer and price: `agency-offer-sheet.md`. Who we call cold: `agency-icp.md`.
The phone script: `agency-cold-call-playbook.md`. **This file is the room you're
already in.**

*Owner-edited. Last updated: 2026-09-08.*

---

## 0. The one job

> **Get permission to build one free, so there's something real to show the
> next person.**

Not a sale. Not a meeting. Permission. The ask is small on purpose, because at
this stage the thing we are short of is not leads — it is **proof**, and a warm
intro is the cheapest proof we will ever buy.

---

## 1. Why this channel beats the phone right now

| | Cold call | Warm intro |
|---|---|---|
| First 10 seconds | Spent proving you're not a scam | Already spent |
| Who you are | A vendor | A director who runs a center on Cecil B. Moore |
| Proof needed | Heavy — they've never heard of you | Light — the person who introduced you *is* the proof |
| Gatekeeper | Usually | Never |
| Cost | 50 dials for one Zoom | One text |

And the asymmetry that matters most: **a cold call that goes badly costs you a
number. A warm intro that goes badly costs you a relationship.** That's why §5
exists — the discipline here is about not burning the room, not about volume.

---

## 2. Who's actually in your network — fill this in before you start

Don't guess at names from this file. Sit down once and write the real list. The
categories are what's transferable; the names are yours.

**Directors and owners you already know**
- Centers you've toured, trained with, subbed for, or sent an overflow family to
- Directors you met at trainings, STARS work, or licensing-related sessions
- The center your own staff came from, and the ones they went to

**Rooms directors are already in** *(verify the current Philly-specific names —
don't cite an org you haven't confirmed)*
- Keystone STARS designators and quality coaches
- Your regional Early Learning Resource Center (ELRC) contacts
- Statewide and regional early-childhood associations
- Local early-childhood nonprofits and training providers
- Facebook / WhatsApp groups for PA and Philly childcare providers

**Vendors who already sell to every center in the city** — they have the list and
the trust, and they are not competitors:
- CACFP food-program sponsors
- Curriculum and assessment vendors
- Classroom-supply reps
- Insurance brokers who write childcare policies
- Licensing/compliance consultants

**Parents and staff.** Your own families and teachers know other centers. A
parent who moved from another center knows that director.

> **One line that does not go in this list: your licensing/certification rep.**
> That relationship is regulatory. Do not turn it into a sales channel.

## 3. The opener — you are not selling

The whole move: **lead with what you built for your own center, and stop.** Let
them ask. Directors ask, because every one of them has the same problem and
almost none of them have solved it.

### Text / DM to a director you know

> "Hey [Name] — random one. I finally rebuilt our site for A Touch of Blessings,
> put the tour booking right on it so parents can book without calling us. It's
> been picking up inquiries overnight, which we were flat missing before.
>
> I'm trying to get a couple more centers on it so I've got real examples. Can I
> build yours? No charge, no catch — I just want to be able to point at it."

### In person / on a call

> "We redid our site — parents can book a tour on it now instead of calling
> while we're with the kids. Made a bigger difference than I expected."
>
> *(stop talking)*
>
> Then, when they ask: "Yeah — I'd actually build yours free. I'm trying to get
> a few real examples up. Want me to just make one and show you?"

### Asking for an intro (not pitching them directly)

> "You know anybody running a center who'd let me redo their site? I'm doing
> them free right now to get examples — I just don't want to cold-call people."

**Why "free" is said out loud immediately here, unlike the cold call:** on a cold
call, a price early kills the frame. In a warm room, an unnamed price makes it
feel like a setup. Name it and the tension drops.

## 4. What you say when they say yes

Same sequence every time.

1. **Build it before you discuss anything.** Their real photos, real ages, real
   hours, real subsidy info, a working tour-booking form. 1–2 days. Use the
   `client-site-build` skill / `site-builder` agent.
2. **Show it, don't send it.** In person or on a screenshare. The reaction is the
   close, and you only get it live.
3. **Then the monthly, once — plainly, and only once:**

   > "The build's free, it's yours, code and domain. The only thing I charge for
   > is keeping it running — a hundred twenty-nine a month covers hosting, any
   > edits you want, and I watch that the form is actually delivering. Six
   > months, then cancel whenever. And it's yours either way."

4. **If they hesitate, do not discount and do not re-pitch.** Say: *"Take it
   live either way — use it, and if it's bringing you tours in a month, we'll
   talk."* A center running your site is proof whether they pay or not, and a
   free user who sees results converts later. **Never argue someone into $129.**
5. **Ask for the testimonial when the first inquiry lands**, not at launch. The
   moment a parent books a tour through it is the moment they'll say something
   real.

## 5. How to not burn the room

This is the part that matters. These are peers in a small city and they talk.

- **One ask per person. Ever.** No follow-up sequence, no "just bumping this."
  A no is a no and the relationship survives it.
- **Never pitch inside a group** — a Facebook or WhatsApp provider group is not a
  lead list. Answer questions, post what you built for your own center when it's
  on topic, and let people DM you. Pitching in the group gets you removed and
  the removal gets talked about.
- **Never use a regulatory or licensing relationship as a channel.** (§2.)
- **Never say a number you haven't measured.** Do not tell another director your
  site "increased enrollment 40%." Say what actually happened: *"Parents book
  tours on it now instead of calling."* If you don't have the number, you don't
  have the number. (`agency-evidence-discipline.md`.)
- **Never badmouth their current site to their face.** On a cold call, naming the
  specific broken thing is the opener. In a warm room it reads as an insult to
  someone who may have built it themselves or paid a friend for it. Lead with
  yours; let the comparison happen in their head.
- **Deliver fast or don't offer.** A free site you take five weeks to finish costs
  more reputation than never offering. If you can't ship in two weeks, don't ask
  yet.

---

## 6. Working it

- **Write the list first** (§2). Twenty names is plenty. Don't start reaching out
  with three.
- **Five reach-outs a day, by text, not email.** These are people who know you —
  an email is more formal than the relationship is.
- **Track every one in the Call Sheet** like any other lead, with the source noted
  as `warm`, so warm and cold conversion stay separable. You cannot tell which
  channel is working if they're mixed.
- **Two free builds live is the goal before the cold-call phase gets serious.**
  Two real Philly centers you can name changes every cold call after it.
- **When one goes live, that's the story for the next ten reach-outs** — and it's
  the fastest legitimate way to get real proof on the public site.
