# ClientForge — Cold Call Playbook

**The phone half.** Who to call is `agency-icp.md`. This file is the offer, the
price, what a won call looks like, the script, and what happens after.

*Owner-edited. Last updated: 2026-07-29.*

---

## 0. The one job on the phone

> **Book a 15-minute Zoom. That's it.**

We do not sell, quote, scope, or close on the cold call. The cold call buys us
fifteen minutes with the shot-caller. **The mockup is the close**, and the mockup
gets revealed live on the Zoom — never emailed, never described on the phone.

If a call ends with anything other than a time on the calendar (or a hard no), it
did not land. "Send me some info" is a no wearing a polite hat — see §5.

---

## 1. The offer ladder — land cheap, expand after

Trust first, margin second. The website is the door, not the business.

| Rung | What | Price | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The door** | Website — built, live, mobile, with a real booking/inquiry form wired to their CRM | **$0–1,000** (free or near-free when the monthly is signed) | This is customer-acquisition cost, not revenue. It buys a relationship and a portfolio piece. |
| **2. The business** | **Care plan — $200–500/mo.** Hosting, edits, updates, form/CRM monitoring, monthly report | **$200–500/mo** | This is the actual business. Recurring, low-effort, high-retention. Every call is aimed here. |
| **3. Expand** | Automations — missed-call text-back, speed-to-lead follow-up, review requests, booking reminders, AI receptionist | project + monthly bump | Sold *after* the site is live and they trust us. Easiest upsell we have. |
| **4. Expand** | Ads management (Eco) | management fee + their spend | Only for businesses already spending. Never sold cold. |
| **5. Expand** | Apps / custom builds | scoped per project | Rare, biggest checks. Comes from existing clients asking, never from a cold call. |

**How we frame it on the Zoom (not the cold call):**
> "The site itself I'm doing for [free / $X] — I'd rather earn the relationship
> than the deposit. What I charge for is keeping it working: $X a month covers
> hosting, any edits you want, and I watch that the form is actually delivering.
> Cancel whenever."

**Rules:**
- **Never quote a price on the cold call.** Same discipline as wholesale: the
  number happens on the call where we can show the work. If pushed, see §5.
- **Never discount the monthly to save a deal.** Discount the build (it's already
  near zero) — the monthly is the business.
- **Never take on a client whose monthly we had to argue them into.** They churn
  at month three and cost more than they paid.

## 2. Before you dial

- **Read the row.** The `pitch_angle` from Clay is the opener. One specific,
  true, agreeable thing about *their* web presence. Generic = dead call.
- **Pull their site up on screen** while it rings. If they say "what's wrong with
  it," you're looking at it.
- **Know the owner's name.** Ask for them by first name — it's the difference
  between a gatekeeper and a conversation.
- **Dial window:** 9:00 AM – 6:00 PM in *their* local time. Never before 9, never
  after 8. For daycares: **9:30–11:00 AM or 1:30–3:00 PM** — before pickup chaos,
  after drop-off chaos.
- **Batch of 50, 90 minutes, no email between calls.** Momentum is the whole game.

## 3. The script — daycare (Tier 1, Philly)

Bracketed = fill in. Do not read it word for word; hit the beats.

**Opener (pattern interrupt + local + peer):**
> "Hey — is this [Director's first name]? … [Name], my name's Yahjair. I actually
> run a daycare here in Philly, A Touch of Blessings over on Cecil B. Moore.
> This isn't a sales thing about supplies or anything — you got thirty seconds?"

*Why it works: "I run a daycare here" is not a claim she has to take on faith,
and it makes this a peer call instead of a vendor call. It's also true.*

**The reason (the specific thing):**
> "So when I built out our own site and our enrollment system — the thing where
> a parent lands on the page and books a tour without anybody calling them back —
> a couple other centers asked me to do theirs. I pulled yours up and
> [the specific, true thing: *there's no way for a parent to book a tour on it* /
> *it doesn't come up right on a phone* / *you don't have a site at all, just the
> Facebook page*]. Is that on purpose, or just never got to it?"

*Then shut up. The pause does the work.*

**The ask (small, specific, time-boxed):**
> "Here's all I want: give me fifteen minutes on Zoom later this week. I'll have
> a version of your site already built to show you — not a pitch deck, the actual
> thing. If you hate it you've lost fifteen minutes. Thursday morning or Friday
> afternoon better?"

**Two options, never "when's good for you."**

**Confirm:**
> "Perfect — Thursday at 10. What's the best email for the invite? … And that's
> the number you'll be at?"

## 4. The script — any other niche

Same skeleton, swap the peer line for the local + specific line:

> "Hey, is this [name]? … [Name], I'm Yahjair — I build websites for [niche]
> businesses here in [city]. Not a cold pitch about SEO or any of that, thirty
> seconds?"
>
> "I was looking at [company] — you've got [X reviews / you're clearly busy],
> but [the specific thing]. That's costing you calls you already paid for."
>
> "Give me fifteen minutes on Zoom. I'll build a version of your site before we
> talk and just show you. Thursday morning or Friday afternoon?"

**For a lead running ads (highest value):**
> "You're running ads on Facebook right now — I saw them. That money's landing on
> a page that [doesn't work on a phone / has no way to actually book]. I'm not
> touching your ads. I want to fix where they land."

## 5. Objections

| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| **"We're not interested."** | "Fair enough — before I let you go, is it that you're happy with the site, or that you just don't have time to deal with it?" *(Second one is a yes in disguise. First one, thank them and mark dead.)* |
| **"How much?"** | "Depends what you need — that's the whole reason for the fifteen minutes. But I'll tell you now, the build is the cheap part, most people are surprised how cheap. Thursday or Friday?" **Never a number on the phone.** |
| **"Send me some info / an email."** | "I can, but honestly the email's going to sit there — everybody's does. Give me the fifteen minutes and I'll have something built to show you. If it's not worth it, you never hear from me again. Thursday morning?" *(If they hold: get the email, send it, and mark `callback` with a date. Never mark it won.)* |
| **"We already have someone."** | "Good — most people don't. Are they keeping it updated, or did they build it and disappear?" *(The second is the norm and it's the opening.)* |
| **"I'm busy / with a kid / with a customer."** | "Totally get it — that's actually the whole reason I called. What's better, later today or tomorrow morning?" *(Set a real time, mark `callback`.)* |
| **"How'd you get my number?"** | "You're listed on Google — that's where I found you. If you'd rather I didn't call again just say so and you're off my list for good." *(And mean it.)* |
| **"Take me off your list."** | "Done — you won't hear from me again. Have a good one." Mark `dead` immediately. No pitch, no last try. |

## 6. After the call

**Booked:**
1. Send the calendar invite **while still on the phone or within 5 minutes.**
2. **Build the mockup before the Zoom** — that's the whole promise. Use the
   `client-site-build` skill / `site-builder` agent. One page, their real photos,
   their real services, a working inquiry form. It does not need to be finished,
   it needs to be *theirs*.
3. Reminder text the morning of.

**Callback:** set the date in the note. Work callbacks before new dials the
next day — a callback converts several times better than a fresh number.

**Dead:** mark it, note why in one line. The "why" is how we fix Clay's filters.

**Won a client:** they go in the dashboard's **Clients** tab. From then on Dyson
handles edits and Eco handles ads — this playbook is done with them.

## 7. The numbers to hold yourself to

Track in the Call Center (dial goal + streak already built).

- **50 dials/day** is the goal. The streak breaks below it.
- Expect roughly: 50 dials → ~10 conversations → ~2–3 Zooms booked → ~1 client.
  **These are planning assumptions, not measured results** — replace them with
  our real numbers after the first 200 dials, and update this line.
- **The only metric that matters early is dials.** Everything downstream is
  script tuning, and you can't tune a script you haven't run 200 times.
- **After 100 dials, review:** which opener got past the first ten seconds, which
  niche picked up most, which pitch angle got the "yeah, I know." Rewrite this
  file with what actually worked. That's the same self-improvement loop the
  agents run — it applies to the operator too.

## 8. Compliance — read once, then follow it

Not legal advice; these are the house rules.

- **Manual dialing only.** Every number is dialed by hand from the Call Sheet.
  No autodialer, no prerecorded or artificial-voice message. This is the simplest
  way to stay clear of the TCPA's autodialer and prerecorded-voice restrictions.
- **If we ever switch to an AI voice agent (Retell) for outbound cold calls, that
  is a different legal posture** — an artificial or prerecorded voice to a cell
  number generally requires prior express consent. Do not flip that on without a
  real compliance review. Retell is fine for *inbound* and for calling people who
  opted in.
- **Business lines are not residential.** The National DNC Registry protects
  residential subscribers; B2B calls to a listed business line generally sit
  outside it. **But** many small-business "office" numbers are the owner's cell —
  treat any number that looks personal with the same care as a residential one.
- **Honor "don't call me" instantly and permanently.** Internal DNC, no
  exceptions, no re-imports. (`agency-icp.md` §7.)
- **Calling hours: 9 AM – 6 PM their local time.** Never outside 8 AM – 9 PM.
- **Recording:** Pennsylvania is an all-party-consent state. **Do not record a
  call without telling them and getting a yes on the recording.** Same rule if
  the other party is in any two-party-consent state. When in doubt, take notes.
- **Never misrepresent.** We are Yahjair from ClientForge. We are not "calling
  from Google," not "with your web host," not returning a call they didn't make.

## 9. Voice

Same as everything else here: direct, specific, honest builder. Real numbers,
real screenshots, no hype. Banned words apply on the phone too —
`unlock`, `supercharge`, `game-changer`, `seamless`, `revolutionary`, `10x`,
`the power of`. Full list: `marketing/context/voice_guide.md`.

Short sentences. Say the true, specific thing about their business, then stop
talking and let them respond.
