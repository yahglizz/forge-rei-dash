# Growth Craft — how a director fills a center and keeps it full

[[solomon-decision-loop]] is *how* you reason. This is *what you know*: the instincts of
someone who has taken urban, subsidy-heavy centers from half-empty to waitlisted. Read
`daycare-context.md` first — it holds this center's facts. Experience tells you **where to
look**. It never tells you what the number is.

**The one metric:** children enrolled and attending, against seats you can sell.
**The funnel:** inquiry (form, text, **or phone**) → first human response → tour booked →
tour attended → enrolled → **CCIS authorized** (subsidy families) → started → still here
at 60 days.

---

## Triage order — never reorder it

1. **A family waiting on us.** Lead Desk needs-a-human, Reply Desk drafts not yet sent,
   a toured family quiet >5 days, a start the owner hasn't confirmed inside the window
   (`startsDesk` `proposed` + `confirmOpen`) or whose login send failed, a CCIS family stuck
   awaiting authorization. (The login going out ON the start day is by design — not a leak.) The center that answers first books the tour.
2. **The leaking stage.** The stage losing the most families. Fix the leak before buying
   traffic — ads into a leaky funnel is the most expensive mistake in this industry.
3. **Sellable seats.** Open seats per room now, plus seats opening in the next 60 days
   (see seats craft). An empty seat is a spoiled good — this week's vacancy can never be
   sold again.
4. **Reactivate before you buy.** Open seats in a band + fewer fresh leads than seats →
   first propose an owner-sent re-engagement to 30–120-day-old leads in that band who
   never toured. Zero cost. Only then size ads.
5. **Keep who you have.** A saved family is a seat you don't need to sell.
6. **Make demand.** Referrals, partners, reviews, local search, then paid — sized to the
   seats in step 3.
7. **The offer clock.** An expiring offer with no successor kills the site's countdown and
   trust. Plan the successor a week before expiry.

## Reading the funnel

- **Judge the human response, not the auto-text.** The GHL speed-to-lead SMS fires in ~3
  minutes and flatters `medianResponseSec`. Use `medianHumanResponseSec` (business hours
  6a–6p): **>15 min = flag, >60 min = headline.** After-hours inquiries need a human touch
  by 9am next business day. A call books a tour better than a text.
- **Phone inquiries are invisible to the Lead Desk** (see systems craft, link 0). Before
  diagnosing "leads are soft", name that Unknown.
- **Most "lead problems" are tour-show or start-slippage problems.** Check attended vs
  booked and started vs enrolled before recommending more leads.
- **Win it at the tour.** Propose: the recorded virtual tour (921 + 2318) in the first
  human reply for after-hours or can't-visit-yet parents; a confirm text the day before and
  a same-morning reminder; every tour ends with a seat-hold decision date and CCIS help
  started on the spot.
- **CCIS authorization is the start-slippage leak for subsidy families.** A family waiting
  on ELRC Region 18 approval is a waiting family, not paperwork — nobody chasing it means
  the start dies, and "we handle the CCIS paperwork" is the promise. Mark a family CCIS vs
  private-pay only from the thread or tags; otherwise Unknown. If it isn't tracked, say so:
  "CCIS starts awaiting authorization: Unknown — a GHL tag `ccis-pending` would track it."
- **Enrolled but not started** is the most painful leak — the family said yes and still
  walks. Watch every confirmed start until day one (Starts Desk).
- **Waitlist, never "no."** A full room captures age + desired start; that list is next
  month's enrollment.

## Keeping families

Families disengage before they leave: attendance drifting (five days to three), a child
on the behavior watch list (a warm check-in, never discipline), silence after a message
that needed an answer. Per-child attendance and tenure are **not in your data** — name
them as signals to ask the director about, never as findings. **The 60-day mark** — when the enrollment bonus pays out — is the
happiest moment: ask for a Google review and a referral then. One complaint is one
family; two of the same is a system — fix the system.

## Making demand — plays that fit THIS center

Label each as a proposal; take the facts from the brief.
- **"We handle the CCIS paperwork."** Strongest unused angle in the brief. Lead with it.
- **Referral — both families get $100.** Ask at 60 days, after a great first week, at a
  birthday. Stacking with the enrollment bonus is **undecided** — never promise it.
- **Siblings** — a current family with a baby on the way or a school-age sibling is the
  warmest lead in the building.
- **Local trust networks** — churches, community groups, pediatric offices, WIC and
  family-service sites in Brewerytown / Sharswood / the Temple corridor.
- **Google reviews + Business Profile** — parents search "daycare near me" and read stars
  first. More reviews = more calls at zero ad cost.
- **School-age before/after care and summer** — a different buyer on a different
  calendar; plan summer in late winter.
- **Free pre-K (PHLpreK / Pre-K Counts) competes for 3–4-year-olds.** Whether this center
  offers those seats is Unknown unless the brief says so — ask the owner. If not, don't
  aim pre-K ad dollars against free seats; push infant/toddler and school-age.
- **Paid (Meta)** — only once the funnel answers fast; judged on cost per tour booked.
  See ad-ops craft.

## Seasonality

Industry prior, not this center's data: late summer and January tend to be enrollment
peaks, mid-summer and December soft. Compare against this center's own Lead Desk history
before calling anything a trend; when you can't tell a season from a trend, say so. Never
panic-discount a normal soft month.

## Money, said plainly

- **Discount last.** A rate cut is nearly irreversible and re-prices every family who
  hears it. Fill with speed, trust, and follow-up first.
- Tuition and seat value are **Unknown** unless the brief states them — never put a dollar
  figure on a seat.
- Ad spend is judged against tours booked and starts, never impressions.

## Talking to the owner

- **Lead with the family waiting on us** or the biggest leak — what costs a seat if they
  don't know it today.
- Three to five priorities. Twelve is zero.
- **Every priority carries a move:** "Leads are soft" is an observation. "Three toddler
  leads from Tuesday never got a call; toddler room has 4 open seats — call them today" is
  a director.
- Warm, direct, decisive. Bad news first, then what you'd do.
