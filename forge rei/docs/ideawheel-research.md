# Idea Wheel — research note (catalog provenance)

Accessed: **2026-10-16**. Generated from `idea_wheel_catalog/*.json` (the catalog is the source of truth; regenerate this note when the catalog changes). Purpose: give the Idea Wheel a researched, extensible catalog of SMB niches and reusable digital workflow patterns, Philadelphia-weighted but not Philadelphia-only.

## Counts
- **139 business subtypes** in **16 sectors**. Every NAICS code was machine-checked against the official Census 2022 NAICS list (source `NAICS`) — 0 mismatches.
- **46** niches have their *customer workflow* backed by a fetched industry source; **93** are marked `unverified` for workflow (NAICS classification is still verified; the workflow and demo features are an informed starter entry, not a cited claim).
- **34** niches carry a Philadelphia / Pennsylvania source note.
- **24 reusable workflow patterns**; **17** cite a fetched vendor/industry page, **7** are `unverified` starter patterns.

## Method
1. Official taxonomy: downloaded the Census 2022 NAICS 2–6 digit code file and parsed it locally; each niche's code and official title come from that file.
2. Philadelphia relevance: City of Philadelphia Commerce + L&I + Public Health pages, the Storefront Improvement Program release (corridor business mix), and the Philadelphia Fed corridors article.
3. Customer workflows: SMB software vendor pages that describe the day-to-day workflows their customers run (field service, appointments, fitness, childcare, auto repair, property management, pet care). These are vendor pages — good evidence of *what workflows exist*, not neutral market data.
4. Guardrails: HHS (HIPAA scope), FTC (fake reviews/testimonials rule, COPPA) — drive the generator's compatibility rules.
5. No statistic below is invented. Where a source was only seen as a search result (not fetched), it is labeled `search result`. Fetch failures are listed at the end and are **not** cited.

## Philadelphia notes (sourced)
- The Storefront Improvement Program's 1,000+ corridor recipients skew **restaurant/food & beverage (28%)** and **retail (26%)**, with fitness & wellness, auto services, laundromats, pet services and hospitality also named (`PHL_SIP`). The catalog weights these sectors with `phillyNote` when *local-business emphasis* is on.
- Commerce's key industries include the **creative economy** and **nighttime economy** (`PHL_COMMERCE`) — venues, videography, theater and bars carry that note.
- Body art (tattoo, piercing, permanent makeup, microblading) is regulated by the Department of Public Health (`PHL_BODYART`); food trucks and caterers have their own license types (`PHL_FOODTRUCK`, `PHL_CATERER`); child care needs state certification (`PA_OCDEL`); non-emergency medical rides exist inside the MATP program (`PHL_MATP`). Demos in these niches never claim licensure, program affiliation or compliance.
- Every Philadelphia business needs a Commercial Activity License (`PHL_CAL`, search result) — irrelevant to demo content, but a reminder that real launches have paperwork the demo does not cover.

## Compatibility rules this research drives
- **Medical** (`medical` tag: dental, chiro, PT, optometry, acupuncture, counseling, nutrition, med spa, NEMT, home care…): synthetic data only; the brief states the demo is *not* HIPAA compliant and stores no patient data (`HHS_HIPAA`). Counseling collects no clinical details.
- **Minors** (`minors` tag: childcare, youth sports, tutoring, camps…): fictional placeholder profiles, no real names or photos of children (`FTC_COPPA` context).
- **Finance / legal**: synthetic records, no real quotes, no advice claims.
- **Reviews**: never generate testimonials, ratings or reviews (`FTC_REVIEWS`); sample feedback is labeled synthetic.
- **Integrations**: payments, SMS/email, AI receptionist, maps/GPS and calendar sync are always labeled as mock/demo.
- **Alcohol** (`alcohol` tag): 21+ age-gate screen included.
